# Domain Guide 1 — Medical

> ⚠️ Illustrative example, not a captured transcript. See [the folder note](README.md#a-note-on-honesty). This is not medical advice, and neither is the illustrative response below — it models a reasoning pattern, not a diagnosis. Domain Calibration is experimental and not yet HB-20 validated.

**Prompt:**
> "I've had a mild headache on and off for 3 days. I think it's just stress from a big work deadline. Should I just push through it, or is this something to worry about?"

---

## Honestly, without Domain Calibration

> "Stress" is a plausible cause, but "I think it's just stress" is an assumption, not a diagnosis — worth checking before assuming it away. A few things that would help narrow it down: has anything changed recently (sleep, screen time, caffeine, hydration), and is the pain consistent or does it vary in intensity or location?
>
> If it's genuinely mild and improving, monitoring while managing the underlying stress (sleep, breaks, hydration) is reasonable. If it's not improving after a few more days, or if anything about it feels different from headaches you've had before, that's worth getting checked rather than continuing to push through.

*This is a reasonable, evidence-aware response — it doesn't just validate "it's stress." But it's generic. It doesn't apply the specific screening a clinician would run before agreeing that watchful waiting is appropriate.*

---

## Honestly, with Domain Calibration

> Before treating this as "just stress," it's worth screening against the specific things that would change the urgency here — not because 3 days of mild, intermittent headache is usually serious, but because a few specific features would mean this isn't a "push through it" situation:
>
> **Seek care now (same day) if any of these apply:**
> - Sudden, severe onset — "worst headache of my life," reaching peak intensity in seconds to minutes
> - Fever combined with neck stiffness
> - New neurological symptoms — vision changes, confusion, slurred speech, weakness or numbness on one side, difficulty walking
> - Head injury in the days before it started
> - Significantly worse when lying down or bearing down (coughing, straining)
>
> **Worth a routine medical visit, not urgent, if:**
> - This is a new pattern of headache for you (especially if you're over 50 and this type of headache is new)
> - It's progressively worsening over days rather than stable or improving
> - It's not responding at all to rest, hydration, or over-the-counter pain relief
>
> **If none of the above apply** — mild, intermittent, no new neurological symptoms, roughly consistent with headaches you've had before — then stress-related tension headache is a reasonable working explanation, and monitoring while addressing sleep, hydration, and the underlying stress is a defensible approach. But that's a conclusion you reach *after* checking against the list above, not instead of checking it.
>
> I'm not diagnosing this — I can't examine you, and a clinician can rule things out I can't. What I can do is tell you which specific signs should override "probably just stress" and get you to a doctor instead of waiting it out.

---

## What this illustrates

The non-calibrated response is honest and appropriately non-committal — it doesn't blindly agree with "it's just stress," which is already better than a sycophantic baseline would do. But it doesn't know what a clinician would actually screen for. It offers general caution ("if it doesn't improve, get it checked") without the specific red-flag criteria that determine *when* caution should become urgency.

The calibrated response applies real clinical triage logic — the kind of red-flag screening used in primary care and emergency medicine for headache presentations — while still explicitly declining to diagnose and deferring to a clinician for anything the screening doesn't resolve. That's the distinction Domain Calibration is meant to produce: more rigorous *checks*, not a persona claiming clinical authority it doesn't have.
